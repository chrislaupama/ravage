## Engine code

RAVAGE ENGINE is derived from GPL-licensed Quake technology (FTEQW, and selected code from Ironwail and QuakeSpasm). The engine is therefore distributed under the GNU General Public License, version 2 or later. The full text is in [LICENSE](../LICENSE).

When importing upstream code:

- Keep every existing copyright and license notice.
- Record where each import came from (repository, commit) in the commit message.
- Review the license of every dependency before adding it, and note any that are not GPLv2-compatible.

## Game content

Maps, models, textures, audio, artwork and branding must be original or properly licensed for RAVAGE. The GPL on the Quake engine source does not grant any right to Quake's commercial assets, so none of them may be committed or distributed.

### Using your own Quake install for development

Developers who legally own Quake may point a local build at their own copy (for example its `id1/` folder) to use as a reference or temporary placeholder while original content is made. The engine can read `.pak` files, `.mdl` models and `.wad` textures for this reason. Those files stay on the developer's machine: `.gitignore` excludes `id1/` and `pak*.pak`, and nothing from them may be committed, packaged, uploaded to CI or shipped. RAVAGE must always run standalone, using only its own redistributable placeholder content.

GPL-licensed Quake *code*, such as the QuakeWorld QuakeC game logic, is different. It can be reused or adapted, provided its copyright notices are kept and the result stays under the GPL.

All content committed so far is original to RAVAGE:

- `content/gfx/palette.lmp` and `content/textures/ravage.wad` are generated by `tools/textures/make_textures.py` from procedural patterns. They contain no third-party artwork and no Quake palette.
- `content/maps/duel_graybox.map` was authored for RAVAGE.
- `content/sound/` is generated by `tools/assets/make_sounds.py` from synthesised waveforms. It contains no third-party recordings.
- `content/models/`: the health, armor, ammo and projectile models and `skin.png` are generated by `tools/assets/make_models.py` from simple solids. The player and weapons come from LibreQuake (see below).
- The game code in `game/` was written for RAVAGE. Its combat and pickup numbers follow QuakeWorld's as a starting point, but no QuakeWorld QuakeC was copied.
- `game/ssqc/engine_defs.qc` and `game/csqc/engine_defs.qc` are generated by the engine's `pr_dumpplatform` command, so they come from the GPL engine source.

### LibreQuake

Some content is derived from [LibreQuake](https://github.com/lavenderdotpet/librequake), whose models, textures and sounds are under the BSD 3-clause license (Copyright © 2019-2023 Contributors to the LibreQuake project). Its QuakeC, `progs.dat` and `pop.lmp` are GPL2, and none of them is used.

- `content/models/player.iqm`: LibreQuake's player mesh, skeleton and animations, converted by `tools/assets/import_librequake.py`.
- `content/models/v_*.md3` (with their `.framegroups` files) and `g_*.iqm`: LibreQuake's first-person weapons with their animations, and its weapon pickups, converted by the same script. The railgun is LibreQuake's super nailgun.
- `content/models/*_skin.png` and `*_skin_luma.png`: LibreQuake's skins for those models, recoloured by the same script.

The BSD license requires its notice to travel with the files, in source and binary form. `content/models/LICENSE.librequake` is LibreQuake's license and `content/models/CREDITS.librequake` its list of contributors. Both are copied into the game data next to the models, and must be included in any release. The LibreQuake revision is recorded as `LIBREQUAKE_COMMIT` in `import_librequake.py`.

The license for original RAVAGE content, tooling and online services has not been decided yet and will be documented here.

## Map tools

TrenchBroom and ericw-tools are GPLv3 programs. RAVAGE uses them as separate programs: they are not linked into the engine and are not committed to this repository. `tools/fetch-ericw-tools.sh` downloads a pinned ericw-tools release (with its own license files) into `deps/`, which git ignores. The RAVAGE TrenchBroom config and FGD in `tools/trenchbroom/RAVAGE` were written for RAVAGE.
